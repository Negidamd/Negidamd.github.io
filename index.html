Research Program

Cholinergic vulnerability and cognition in Parkinson's disease and Alzheimer's disease and related dementias

Parkinson's disease (PD), dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB), and Alzheimer's disease (AD) are heterogeneous in their clinical features, underlying pathology, and progression. Enrolling biologically diverse patients into trials of target-specific therapies can dilute treatment effects. Degeneration of the nucleus basalis of Meynert (Ch4), which supplies most cholinergic input to the cortex and modulates attention, is shared across all three diseases and is the biological rationale for cholinesterase inhibitors—yet response to these drugs varies widely. My program develops multimodal imaging markers of Ch4 degeneration to identify the patients most likely to benefit from cholinergic therapies, from cholinesterase inhibitors to muscarinic agonists, Ch4 deep brain stimulation, and gene therapy.

Research Contributions

01 · Imaging

Measuring Ch4 Degeneration In Vivo

Using probabilistic cytoarchitectonic maps, I quantified Ch4 integrity from standard MRI and identified a subtype of PD mild cognitive impairment defined by MRI evidence of Ch4 degeneration, released as an open tool. In 92 participants from VCU clinics, Ch4 gray-matter density and Ch4 free water were the only basal forebrain measures independently associated with MoCA scores; together they discriminated PD dementia from PD without dementia (AUC 0.93; preprint under review). Ch4 connectivity with the cingulo-opercular network statistically mediated the link between Ch4 degeneration and cognition.

T1 · Free-water diffusion · Resting-state fMRI
02 · EEG

Complementing MRI with EEG

EEG is inexpensive, repeatable, and widely available. In the NIH R21 AG077469 study, I analyzed resting-state EEG in Lewy body disease and built a machine-learning model that detected cognitive fluctuations with an AUC of 0.86 (Alzheimer's Research & Therapy, 2026), releasing the feature-extraction pipeline as an open-source toolbox. This work, with the Ch4 MRI pipeline, contributed pilot data to the funded R01 NS142622 on cholinergic degeneration and cognitive fluctuations.

Resting-state EEG · Cognitive fluctuations · Machine learning
03 · Modeling

Disease Progression Modeling

Using PPMI, I modeled ten-year progression milestones across clinical, pathological, and data-driven PD subtypes; the diffuse-malignant subtype reached milestones fastest (npj Parkinson's Disease, 2026). To identify these patients prospectively, I developed disease-duration–specific percentiles applicable within five years of diagnosis (Mov Disord Clin Pract, 2026), released an open-source subtype calculator, and characterized the genetics of these subtypes (Genes, 2026). Ongoing Work

Ch4-salience connectivity relates to cognition beyond atrophy and free water

Resting-state fMRI in 97 participants (16 controls, 81 with PD) showed Ch4 connectivity with salience, action-mode, and language networks declining across PD cognitive stages (FDR q < 0.05). Salience connectivity added 8.6 percentage points of explained variance in cognition beyond Ch4 gray-matter density and free water, although the effect weakened in sensitivity analyses.

Pending Publication

Ch4 atrophy and free water independently predict cognition in PD

We combined T1-weighted and free-water diffusion MRI in 92 participants (controls, PD-NC, PD-MCI, PDD). Ch4 gray-matter density was lowest and free water highest in PDD. Only these two Ch4 measures independently predicted cognition, and together they separated PDD from PD without dementia (exploratory AUC 0.93).

medRxiv preprint, 2026 →

Published

Low Ch4 gray-matter density marks a more severe, faster-declining PD-MCI group

Using voxel-based morphometry and probabilistic Ch4 maps in 162 PPMI participants with PD-MCI, we identified 40 with low Ch4 density (below −1 SD). They had worse MDS-UPDRS, autonomic, and olfactory scores and reached cognitive milestones sooner (log-rank p = 0.002). The classifier is released as an open research tool.

Published

Slower resting EEG rhythms flag cognitive fluctuations in Lewy body disease

Eyes-closed resting EEG in 53 patients across the Lewy body spectrum. Patients with fluctuations had lower dominant frequency, dominant-alpha prevalence, and individual alpha peak frequency after adjusting for age, sex, and MoCA; an elastic-net model detected fluctuations with AUC 0.86. The schematic shows the proposed Ch4-thalamocortical mechanism.

TBI mortality across 29 countries does not follow a development gradient

This prospective cohort enrolled 2,165 patients with operative or nonoperative TBI at 100 hospitals. In inverse-probability-weighted mixed-effects models, mortality showed no dose-response with the Human Development Index; injury mechanism, demographics, and prehospital access drove the disparities. Published

Diffuse-malignant PD progresses fastest and could halve trial sample sizes

Over 10 years of PPMI follow-up, we compared clinical, pathological, and data-driven subtypes on milestones across six functional domains using Cox regression. Diffuse-malignant patients reached 63.0% of milestones (PIGD 55.6%, body-first 54.0%), and in trial simulations enrolling them cut the required sample size by about 50%.

npj Parkinson's Disease, 2026 → CC BY 4.0 · cropped

Published

Year-specific percentiles enable real-time PD subtyping for trial enrollment

Percentile thresholds for motor, cognitive, RBD, and autonomic scores, set for each of the first 5 years after diagnosis (PPMI, N = 1,030), classify patients prospectively each year. Diffuse-malignant PD reached a first progression milestone fastest (HR 3.03, 95% CI 2.30–3.97, vs mild motor-predominant).

Mov Disord Clin Pract, 2026 → CC BY-NC-ND 4.0

Published

LRRK2 and GBA1 map onto different PD subtypes

Variant frequencies were compared across four subtyping frameworks in 1,390 genotyped PPMI patients with FDR correction. LRRK2 variants were enriched in α‑synuclein SAA-negative PD (37.1% vs 10.2%) and GBA1 variants in diffuse-malignant PD (14.0% vs 5.9% in mild motor-predominant); the tremor/gait split showed no signal that survived correction.

Negida-DTI-ALPS

Diffusion MRI · Open Source

DTI-ALPS pipeline for quantifying perivascular (glymphatic) diffusivity, using ANTs SyN nonlinear registration in place of the FSL-FLIRT tensor registration of the standard alps.sh toolbox, with T1-mediated boundary-based registration that corrects ALPS ROI misplacement in high head-rotation cases. Validated against the reference implementation across 106 diffusion-MRI datasets (r = 0.96 for mean ALPS). MIT-licensed.

Diversity, Equity & Global Impact

My commitment to diversity and equity in science is demonstrated through sustained action across multiple dimensions. As founder of a research training academy serving 35,000+ trainees across 20+ countries—predominantly in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs)—I have worked to dismantle barriers to research participation for underrepresented communities in the Middle East, Africa, and South Asia. I developed the first neuroimaging analysis course in Arabic, addressing a critical language gap that excluded thousands of researchers from advanced training opportunities.

As co-founder of the Global NeuroSurg collaborative (2,000+ investigators, 50+ countries), I have championed inclusive research models that ensure investigators from LMICs are not merely data collectors but equal scientific partners with authorship and leadership roles.
